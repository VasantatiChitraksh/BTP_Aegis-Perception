Figure 3 | printed page 3 | fig:matched-128-vehicles
Source: sections/00_vehicle_results.tex
Matched vehicle comparison: source-size YOLOv8n, a $128\times128$ resize-only canvas with YOLOv8n, and Restormer on the same $128\times128$ canvas followed by YOLOv8n. A common display crop retains both annotated vehicles and every saved prediction. TP/FP/FN use confidence 0.25 and class-aware IoU 0.50. Restoration recovers the truck relative to resize-only in snow, but it remains missed in fog, rain and smoke; the extra rain prediction in the resize control disappears. These saved CPU FP32 diagnostic overlays are not Nano benchmark frames.

Figure 7 | printed page 7 | fig:architecture
Source: sections/05_method.tex
Architectures evaluated on the paired benchmark. Restormer uses transformer blocks (TBs) at all four scales. LiteWeather v2 uses local residual blocks at full and half resolution and attention at quarter and eighth resolution. Its soft prompt mixture is added before the eighth-scale transformer blocks. Arrows between scales represent down/up-sampling. Dashed arrows show encoder-to-decoder concatenation. Each network predicts a residual added to the degraded RGB image.

Figure 8 | printed page 8 | fig:size
Source: sections/06_restoration.tex
Performance across six trained systems. \textbf{(a)} Macro PSNR versus parameter count. Bars are 95\% cluster-bootstrap intervals conditional on the fixed checkpoints and test groups. The dashed segment connects the measured quality--size frontier. \textbf{(b)} Weather-balanced PSNR gain over the degraded input. Smoke has the smallest gains for all six systems.

Figure 9 | printed page 8 | fig:weather-psnr
Source: sections/06_restoration.tex
The completed six-model benchmark as a weather-by-model comparison. Restormer leads rain, snow and smoke; PromptIR leads fog. Each cell is the recorded balanced test PSNR, rounded to two decimals. The underlying comparison uses full precision.

Figure 10 | printed page 9 | fig:restoration-gain-histograms
Source: sections/06_restoration.tex
Distributions of per-image PSNR improvement over degraded input for Restormer and RAMiT, using the same 2,334 held-out paired images per model. Histograms use 2 dB bins and normalize each model within each weather to 100\%. Each image has equal weight here; these descriptive distributions are distinct from the group- and weather-balanced benchmark means. Restormer's median gain is higher in all four weather groups; smoke retains a negative-gain tail.

Figure 11 | printed page 10 | fig:joint
Source: sections/07_continuation.tex
Completed continuation experiments relative to the matched further-training control. Milli-decibels keep the small differences readable with two decimal places. The control improves over the original checkpoint by 118.97 mdB, while the best additional gain is 28.81 mdB. No arm passes the validation replacement rule.

Figure 12 | printed page 10 | fig:continuation-gate
Source: sections/07_continuation.tex
Validation replacement criterion for the eight continuation arms. Panels compare selected checkpoints with the epoch-51 starting checkpoint and matched continuation control. Both improvements must reach the dashed $+0.10$ dB threshold before applying SSIM and per-weather retention criteria.

Figure 15 | printed page 12 | fig:matched-128-people
Source: sections/08_comparison_galleries.tex
The same three labelled people under four added-weather conditions, using the identical pipeline and thresholds as Figure~\ref{fig:matched-128-vehicles}. Full saved overlays are shown. Figure 16 | printed page 13 | fig:nano-runtime
Source: sections/09_edge.tex
Original Jetson Nano throughput and board input energy. Each point is one weather condition. The lines span conditions within a workload. Live application FPS and replay FPS have different timing boundaries. Energy uses integrated board input power divided by processed frames.

Figure 17 | printed page 14 | fig:nano-latency-histograms
Source: sections/09_edge.tex
Measured per-frame pipeline latency on the Jetson Nano for YOLOv8n alone and Restormer ($128\!\times\!128$ restoration canvas) followed by YOLOv8n. The saved labelled replay uses 24 unique images per weather and five excluded warm-up frames. The raw path repeats the images to record 240/216/216 timed frames; the restored path records 24 per weather, with each window lasting at least 30 seconds. Histograms use 20 ms bins and normalize each run to 100\%; dashed lines mark medians. The two rows deliberately use different latency ranges. Timing covers synchronized restoration/resize and detector preprocessing, inference and NMS; capture, decoding, weather synthesis, scoring and preview are excluded.
